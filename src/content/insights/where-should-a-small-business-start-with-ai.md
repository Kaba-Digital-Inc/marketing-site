Most small business owners are told to "use AI" and are rarely shown where. The result is a pile of subscriptions nobody owns and a workflow that still runs the old way. The fix is not more tools. It is choosing the right first workflow.

## What makes a good first AI workflow

A good first project passes five tests. If a candidate fails two or more, pick a different one.

1. **It happens often.** Daily or weekly beats quarterly. Frequency is what turns small savings into real ones.
2. **It follows rules most of the time.** Invoice matching, appointment scheduling, lead triage, and report drafting all have a pattern. Work that is pure judgment is a poor first target.
3. **Someone dislikes doing it.** The people closest to the work know where the time goes. They also decide whether a new system gets used or quietly ignored.
4. **You can measure it.** Hours per week, errors per month, time to respond. If you cannot name the number now, you will not know if it improved.
5. **A mistake is recoverable.** Start where a wrong answer costs a correction, not a lawsuit. You can move to higher-stakes work once the system has earned trust.

## Examples that usually pass

- Sorting and answering routine customer emails, with a person approving anything unusual
- Pulling data from invoices or forms into your accounting or CRM system
- Drafting quotes or proposals from a template and a short intake
- Summarising meetings and turning them into tasks
- Preparing the weekly report that someone assembles by hand

## Projects to avoid first

- **Anything that touches sensitive data before you know where it goes.** Check what each tool stores and who can see it before you connect customer records.
- **Fully autonomous decisions about money or people.** Keep a human approval step until the system has a track record.
- **A custom build when a configured tool would do.** See our note on [build versus buy](/insights/build-or-buy-ai-tools/).

## How to check your choice in two weeks

You do not need to commit to a build to find out whether a workflow is a good candidate. Spend a short, fixed period on it:

- **Days 1 to 3:** Shadow the work. Write down every step, who does it, which tools are involved, and how long it takes.
- **Days 4 to 6:** Collect twenty real examples, including the messy ones. Real cases show whether the rules actually hold.
- **Days 7 to 10:** Try the simplest tool that could work on those examples and record where it succeeds and where it fails.
- **Days 11 to 14:** Decide. Either the numbers justify building it properly, or you learned cheaply that it is not ready.

This is the same shape as our fixed-scope [AI-Native Audit](/ai-native/): map the work, rank the opportunities by return and effort, and give you a written decision on each.

## A note on expectations

The first project should be small enough to finish in weeks, not months. Its job is to prove the approach and teach your team how to work with these systems. Once one workflow is running and measured, the second one is much easier to choose and much easier to staff.
